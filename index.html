Human Motion Generation from Text, Scene, and Trajectory on NymeriaPlus

Lucas Pilla Pimentel
Technical University of Munich
Paper (PDF) Code

Abstract

Human motion generation conditioned jointly on text, scene, and trajectory remains underexplored, largely because few real-world datasets pair motion with both scene context and language annotations at scale. We address this by training on NymeriaPlus, a large-scale dataset of real-world egocentric motion capture with paired scene and text annotations. We propose a diffusion model in which text, scene, and a goal position are encoded as tokens, concatenated onto the motion sequence, processed together by a single self-attention transformer, and rolled out autoregressively to generate long motions. Each condition is dropped independently during training in the style of classifier-free guidance, so at sampling time any subset of the three can be supplied, omitted, or guided at its own strength. Qualitative rollouts on held-out test sequences show motion that follows the text, respects the scene, and reaches the goal, and a per-condition guidance ablation shows that each guidance weight controls its own condition.

Method

CFG Ablation

We generate a two-segment rollout in a simple synthetic scene with a single box-shaped obstacle (“The person walks forward and raises their right hand.” then “The person lowers their right hand and raises their left hand.”) and sweep the guidance scale of one condition from 0 to 3 while keeping the other two fixed at 1. Increasing the text guidance makes the described arm raises more pronounced, increasing the scene guidance keeps the trajectory clear of the obstacle, and increasing the goal guidance moves the rollout further toward the waypoints.
